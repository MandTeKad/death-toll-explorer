Mass Shootings: Definitions Matter

The number of “mass shootings” depends entirely on how you define them. The Gun Violence Archive (4+ shot) counts hundreds per year; the Mother Jones / Congressional Research Service definition (indiscriminate public attacks, 3–4+ killed) counts a handful. Most GVA-counted incidents are gang-related, domestic, or crime-related — not the public rampages people picture.

FBI Violent Crime Breakdown (2023)

Of ~1.22 million violent crimes reported in 2023, aggravated assault accounts for the vast majority. Murder, rape, and robbery make up the rest. Overall violent crime fell 3% from 2022.

Crime Rate Changes, 2022 → 2023

Year-over-year percent change in major crime categories. Most violent and property crimes declined, but motor vehicle theft surged 12.6%.

Registered Sex Offenders — Top 10 States

Total registered sex offenders by state (Jan. 2025). Texas leads with ~75,700, followed by California (~60,600) and New York (~43,000). Nationally, there are ~802,066 registrants, up ~6% since 2019.

Sex Offenders Per 100K Residents — Top 10 States

Oregon has the highest per-capita rate at ~790 per 100,000 residents, more than 3× the national average of ~242. Arkansas and Montana also rank far above average.

Recidivism: Rearrest Within 5 Years by Crime Type

Property offenders have the highest rearrest rate (78%), while homicide offenders have the lowest (60%). Sex offenders fall in the middle at ~67% (for any offense, not just sex crimes). First arrested before age 18 = 80%+ rearrest rate.

Sex Offender Recidivism: Rearrest vs. Reconviction vs. Return to Prison

Within 9 years, ~67% of sex offenders are rearrested (for any offense), ~50% reconvicted, and ~40% return to prison. However, for same-type sex offenses specifically, only 7.7% are rearrested—3× higher than other released prisoners, but far below popular perception.

Child Sexual Abuse: Risk Factors

Children in foster care are 4× more likely to experience sexual abuse than the general population. Children in group homes face 28× the risk. CPS receives a report of child sexual abuse every 9 minutes.

Violent Crime Arrests by Race

FBI arrest data shows White individuals account for ~51.5% of violent crime arrests and Black individuals ~44.2%, despite Black Americans being ~13.7% of the U.S. population. These are arrest figures, not convictions, and do not include Hispanic/Latino as a separate racial category (FBI tracks ethnicity separately from race).

Homicide: Offenders & Victims by Race

Black Americans are disproportionately represented as both homicide offenders (~54.8%) and victims (~55.8%). About 89% of Black homicide victims are killed by Black offenders; ~79% of White victims by White offenders. The Black homicide victimization rate is 21.3 per 100K vs. 3.2 per 100K for White Americans (BJS, 2023).

Imprisonment Rate per 100K by Race (2022)

FBI Rape Definition: Legacy vs. Revised

The FBI’s legacy definition (used until 2013) counted only forcible penile-vaginal rape of a female. The 2013 revision broadened the definition to include all penetration without consent, any gender. This caused a statistical spike that does not reflect an actual increase in crime.

The Justice Funnel: From Rape to Incarceration

Of an estimated 465,483 rapes per year, only ~31% are reported to police. Of those, ~26,500 lead to arrest (5.7% of estimated). Only ~3,258 result in felony conviction (0.7%), and ~2,795 are incarcerated (0.6%). The vast majority of rapists face no criminal consequences.

False Rape Accusation Rates by Study

Published false accusation rates vary wildly depending on methodology. Rigorous academic studies (Lisak 2010, meta-analyses) converge around 2–10%. Kanin (1994) is an outlier at 41%, criticized for relying on police polygraph threats that may have coerced recantations. The FBI “unfounded” rate (~5–8%) includes cases dropped for insufficient evidence, not just proven false reports.

The False Accusation Spectrum

The wide range (2–45%) reflects different definitions, sample sizes, and methodologies. The lowest figures (2–3%) count only cases definitively proven false. Mid-range estimates (5–8%) include “unfounded” cases. The highest figures (Kanin’s 41–50%) used polygraph threats and small, non-representative samples from a single jurisdiction.

Wrongful Sexual Assault Convictions

According to the National Registry of Exonerations, 53% of wrongful sexual assault convictions involved a false accusation—the highest of any crime category. DNA exonerations have freed hundreds of men wrongly convicted of rape, with Black men disproportionately affected.

False accusation rates are notoriously difficult to measure because (a) many genuine rapes go unreported, (b) “unfounded” is a police classification that doesn’t always mean “proven false,” and (c) small sample sizes produce volatile estimates.
