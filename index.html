Mass Shootings: Definitions Matter

The number of “mass shootings” depends entirely on how you define them. The Gun Violence Archive (4+ shot) counts hundreds per year; the Mother Jones / Congressional Research Service definition (indiscriminate public attacks, 3–4+ killed) counts a handful. Most GVA-counted incidents are gang-related, domestic, or crime-related — not the public rampages people picture.

Political ideology: ~75% of mass shooters show no clear political affiliation. Of the ~25% with identifiable ideology, the U.S. Extremist Crime Database (1990–2020) finds ~84% far-right and ~16% far-left among ideologically motivated killings specifically. This does NOT mean 84% of mass shooters are right-wing — it means that among the small subset with identifiable political motives, right-wing extremism predominates. Notable ideological cases include El Paso (2019, white supremacist), Buffalo (2022, white supremacist), Orlando (2016, Islamist), and San Bernardino (2015, Islamist). The vast majority of mass shooters are motivated by personal grievances, mental health crises, or workplace/domestic disputes, not politics.
